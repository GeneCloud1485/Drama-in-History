
# Social Encounters
> Only meaningful or substantial social encounters should be played out. If it's trivial engagement, just talk it through. If there's nothing at stake, nothing to roll for.

## Starting Tone
> If the scene is planned, and/or the cast has a determined disposition... then use that. Otherwise, establish a starting tone based on character preconceptions/prejudices, mood, and any character history, reputation, status or rank that applies.

### Modifying the Start Tone

1. History
2. Preconceptions
4. Disposition, mood or situation
5. Status
6. Reputation

Each of the modifiers can add base dice or penalty dice into the roll for starting the tone.

| Friendly                | 11   | 2           | +4Bd   |

## Goal of the Conversation
> Should that be clear at the outset or something that's established partway through the role-playing?

The goal of the conversation will determine whether it's a short, medium, or long series. 


## Role Play
> Play out the scene, but don't quite reach the conclusion. Then, build the pool, roll dice, and see how the scene played out.

## Build the pool and roll
- Pick the most appropriate skill
- Add modifiers from the starting tone
- Modifiers for the performance & role play
- Add modifier for what is at stake
- Roll the pool, count successes
- Look for patterns

## Conclude the Scene

## Series of Conversations
> If more than one stage of a conversation is needed, then it can be completed on the spot. More likely, time will pass and the second series can be played out. In general, a different skill, tactic, or approach should be taken with the second or third series.
